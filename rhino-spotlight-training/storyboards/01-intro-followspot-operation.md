## Full narration script (for recording)

> Introduction to Followspot Operation.
>
> A followspot is a hand-operated light that keeps a moving performer in light. Every move it makes comes from one person: the operator.
>
> Followspots usually run from out in the house: a booth, a balcony, or a chair rigged in the truss. Elevated positions require working-at-height training.
>
> Most followspots share the same core controls, though layouts vary by model. The light source creates the beam. The dowser shutters or fades it. The iris sets the beam's size. The color boomerang drops color filters into the beam. Zoom and focus set the size range and the sharpness of the edge. The sight lets you aim before the light is open. And the handles pan and tilt the spot.
>
> On a show, you take cues over a headset. A cue tells you who to pick up, the size, and the color — then ends with "Go."
>
> *[Second voice]* Spot one, stand by… pick up center, full body, frame two… Go.
>
> Be ready before the go, and keep the performer in the light.
>
> Later lessons cover each control, cue calling, and clean pick-ups. Before running any followspot, get trained on that model, and follow your venue's procedures.

Delivery notes: calm, even, instructional; brief pause (~0.5 s) between each control in Scene 4.
